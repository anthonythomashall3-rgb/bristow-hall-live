Notes:

The Bristow-Hall Rule (C1021) signals a recession’s start when, in the data as first published, seven measures turn together (insured unemployment, job openings against claims, stocks, production, the Baa yield, the unemployment rate, payrolls), or the unemployed count and claims reach 12-month highs with stocks down on the year, or the funds rate jumps 1.5 points in a month, or job losers rise 15 log points on the year after an inverted yield curve. It signals the end when initial claims fall from their peak and a second measure confirms it.

The index shows how near a call is (1.00 = recession); during a recession it adds a damage grade (0–10, log scale; 1929–33 = 10) from unemployment, real GDP and industrial production.

Real-time data only: each call uses the data as published that day.
