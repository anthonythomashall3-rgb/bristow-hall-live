The backstop tier

Three outside rules, each with zero false alarms on the programme's own real-time record 1948–2026 (collection 249, 20 September 2026), run beside the Bristow-Hall Rule. Each is watched on its own (the shadow readings below). Together they are also an opener (collection 256, 20 September 2026): once their union has been ON for thirteen consecutive weeks — one quarter — and the Rule stands closed, the tier opens an episode tagged b. Replayed causally over 1956–2026 the gated opener changes nothing and false-alarms never; the plain, ungated opener was refused (it would have moved the 1990 and 2024 calls outside the window). The tier is the floor: if the core ever misses a recession, one of these turns on three to five months after the peak, and a quarter later the episode opens.

The Rule stands closed since 2024-09-26. Backstops on: none. Opener: union off (last call 2020-07-02; 8 calls since 1967, every one inside an episode the Rule had already opened).

SOS_NSA: 52-week change of the 26-week average of the unadjusted insured unemployment rate (continued claims ÷ covered employment), line +0.20 pp — the programme's declared form of O'Trakoun & Scavette (2025). LMSI30: states (of the 50 + DC) whose 13-week average insured rate is at least 0.20 pp above a year earlier, from the Department of Labor's ETA 539 file, line 30 — the programme's declared form of the San Francisco Fed's Labor Market Stress Indicator (2025). CFNAI: the Chicago Fed National Activity Index three-month average below −0.70, the Chicago Fed's own rule. Michez: Michaillat and Saez's rule, the smaller of the unemployment indicator (Sahm form, first prints) and the vacancy indicator (the twelve-month high of the three-month mean vacancy rate over its value) at or above 0.29 — a shadow reading on the scoreboard, not in the opener.
