National Disturbance Chronology

Every national slowdown since 1948 that did not reach the recession bar: dated by one fixed rule and graded on the same damage scale as recessions.

At a glance

The line is the four NBER coincident series (payroll employment, industrial production, real income less transfers, real manufacturing and trade sales) combined by the Conference Board method and measured against a smooth trend. A disturbance is a stretch in which the line falls (activity grows more slowly than its trend, or shrinks) that stopped short of a recession.

Grade: the program's damage grade (labor, output and production), the same arithmetic the Damage Index uses for recessions, applied to each episode's own dates. No labels; the number is the grade. "Settled" means the trough is at least two years old; newer dates can still move as data are revised.

How a disturbance is decided

1. Dated

A contraction of the coincident composite relative to its trend (Hodrick-Prescott, λ = 133,107.94, the OECD's monthly value), with peaks and troughs found by the Bry-Boschan rule. Windows come from the OECD's settings (minimum phase 5 months) plus any shorter window, down to 2 months, that passes test 3. Length alone never decides.

2. Not a recession

The window contains no NBER recession month (nor Paper 1's April–August 2024 recession). A slowdown that runs straight into a recession is that recession's run-up, not a separate episode.

3. Real

The composite falls at least 1.0 percentage point against its trend from peak to trough.

4. Below the recession bar

The Sahm indicator (three-month average unemployment rate minus its 12-month low) stays under 0.50, the Sahm rule's own trigger, from the peak to three months after the trough. Every NBER recession since 1948 has reached at least 1.47.

Jobless-recovery disturbance

A contraction that begins within 24 months after an NBER trough, while payroll employment is still below its pre-recession peak in every month, in which unemployment rises again by less than 1.0 point (measured from its low inside the window). It is judged against this bar instead of test 4.

Fixed in advance

Nothing is tuned to the episodes. The same rule runs each month on current FRED data; the code and every ruling behind it are in the program's records.

Close calls and what is not here

Several episodes often called slowdowns leave no mark on the composite at all and so are not here: the 1987 stock-market crash, the 1998 financial crisis, the 2004–05 soft patch and the March 2023 bank failures. Financial stress that never reaches jobs, output, income and sales is not a disturbance by this rule.
