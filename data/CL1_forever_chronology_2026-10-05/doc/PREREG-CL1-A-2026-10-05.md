# PREREG CL1-A — a recession chronology from fixed families at their own quiet records (5 October 2026)

Collection: `data/CL1_forever_chronology_2026-10-05/` in the repository `bristow-hall-live`, branch
`claude/recession-detection-requirements-b35y09` (a cloud session; the Mac's numbered collections were not reachable, so
this collection carries a letter prefix and takes a number from `_CLAIMS/claim.py next` when it is copied to the Mac).
Nothing here touches the live tool (collection 105), the site, collection 45 or the Knowledge Base.

This file is written and committed **before** any CL1-A design is scored. Its git commit time is the evidence.

## 1. The standard (Anthony, 5 October 2026)

- One alert: a recession has started (the tool opens) or has ended (the tool closes).
- The board N+P1: the NBER's twelve postwar recessions and Paper 1's 2024 (peak 2024-04, trough 2024-08).
- Calls: inside [−365, +31] days of the end of the peak month (W12). Troughs (closes): inside [−31, +31] days of the end
  of the trough month, with no close before that window (a false stop).
- False alarm: an opening attributed to no recession zone [end of peak month − 365 days, end of trough month], counted
  from 1 January 1948. Openings after 31 August 2024 count as false alarms (strict) and are listed as pending.
- Also reported, for comparison with the program's earlier work: W24 (−730) and W4 (−122).
- Scorer: `harness/score.py` (self-test pins it to synthetic cases and to v3.76's own published record:
  13/13 inside W12, 0 false alarms, 13/13 closes inside ±31; 12/13 inside W3 with 1980 at −146 days).

## 2. Why this design, and why it differs from what failed before

The program's records (538 C11–C15, 547, fixed-rule search, 952 outside tools) show one failure above all others: a method
that chooses its routes (which signals, which pairs) from the recessions it has already seen picks routes that fire early in
the next run-up (1966–67, spring 1979, 2022–23), and a rule whose levels are chosen from the record is fitted however simple
it looks. CL1-A therefore chooses nothing from the recessions:

1. **The families are fixed in advance from the NBER's own definition** (a significant decline in activity spread across the
   economy: employment, production, income, spending) **and from the mechanism atlas** (layoffs, unemployment, production,
   markets, credit, money). Every family is in natural units. No family is admitted or dropped by its record.
2. **Every line is the family's own quiet record**: the worst reading the family has ever shown outside every known
   recession, using only days at least three years old at the moment of reading (so no unannounced peak can sit within two
   years of them) and only recessions whose trough the NBER had announced by that day. A reading at 1.00 is "as bad as any
   expansion has ever been". Record theory then says what the line's false-alarm rate is (about 1/(n+1) per year for one
   family with n quiet years), which is why one family alone cannot be the rule.
3. **Combination by independent witnesses**: a recession is called when k distinct families stand at or beyond their own
   quiet records at the same time. Two families from different parts of the economy breaking their expansion records together
   is the definition's "spread across the economy" made mechanical. No pair is privileged.

Differences from the closest earlier attempts:
- 538's "rules with no knowledge" (§3.3 of RECORD-538-FIXED-RULES) flagged signals in their top 15–2.5 % of all earlier
  weeks; CL1-A's bar is the quiet record itself (the 100th percentile of quiet time), never a quantile, and its window is W12,
  not W4.
- 538's C11–C15 chose routes (pairs and triples) by how well they separated past recessions; CL1-A has no routes.
- 547's channel tools picked channels and lines per cut; CL1-A picks neither.

## 3. The families (fixed; computed only from what was published on each day)

| | Family | Reading (natural units) | Frequency, known on | Data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Layoffs | initial claims, SA, 4-week mean: log rise above its lowest 4-week mean of the prior 52 weeks | weekly, release day | DOL first prints (repo collections 45, 59, 105 cache), ALFRED ICSA vintages from 2009 |
| F2 | Insured unemployment | insured unemployment rate, SA, 4-week mean minus its lowest 4-week mean of the prior 52 weeks (points) | weekly, release day | collection 59 real-time SA first prints 1949–1983; DOL first prints after |
| F3 | Unemployment | real-time Sahm gap (3-month mean of the unemployment rate minus its lowest 3-month mean of the prior 12 months) | monthly, release day | ALFRED UNRATE vintages from 1960; as printed 1948–1961 (cloud inbox, EI/E&E) |
| F4 | Jobs | nonfarm payrolls: log fall below the highest of the prior 12 months | monthly, release day | ALFRED PAYEMS vintages from 1955; as printed 1948–1955 |
| F5 | Production | industrial production: log fall below the highest of the prior 12 months | monthly, release day | ALFRED INDPRO vintages from 1927 |
| F6 | Equity market | S&P 500: log fall below the highest close of the prior 250 trading days | daily, next morning | daily closes from 1928 (private repository; never committed here) |
| F7 | Credit | Moody's Baa minus Aaa yield: rise above its lowest of the prior 52 weeks (points) | monthly to 1985 (next month), daily from 1986 (next morning) | FRED BAA, AAA, DBAA, DAAA (never revised) |
| F8 | Money | deepest inversion of the prior 12 months: max(0, −(10-year − 3-month bill)) (points) | monthly to 1961, daily from 1962 | FRED GS10, TB3MS, DGS10, DTB3 (never revised) |

Each family f has a record ratio, computed on each day t:

  ρ_f(t) = (x_f(t) − median_q) / (record_q − median_q),

where median_q and record_q are the median and maximum of x_f over quiet days known at t (quiet: not inside
[peak-month end − 365 days, trough-month end + 365 days] of any recession whose trough was announced by t; and at least
three years before t). A family needs at least 10 quiet years before its ratio is read (until then it is dark).
For the frozen in-sample record (T1), median_q and record_q use every quiet day 1948–2026 (G1; reported, never used to choose).

## 4. The designs (12, all scored; none tuned)

Opening rule: on day t the tool opens when at least k families have ρ_f ≥ 1.00, each of them continuously for at least h
days (h = 1: the day itself; h = 14: two weeks). Families that are dark do not count.

| Design | Families | k | h |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 / A2 | all eight | 2 | 1 / 14 |
| A3 / A4 | all eight | 3 | 1 / 14 |
| A5 / A6 | labor-anchored: at least one of F1–F3, and at least one other family (any of the eight) | 2 | 1 / 14 |
| A7 / A8 | labor only: at least one of F1–F3 | 1 | 1 / 14 |
| A9 / A10 | labor only: at least two of F1–F3 | 2 | 1 / 14 |
| A11 / A12 | any one family (the single-family baseline record theory warns about) | 1 | 1 / 14 |

Re-arm: after a close, no opening for 182 days.

Closing rule (the trough call), the same for every design: after an opening, let M be the highest 4-week mean of initial
claims since the opening. The tool closes on the first weekly release on which the 4-week mean has stood at least 10 percent
(log 0.105) below M for four consecutive releases, and at least 91 days after the opening. (Principle: the NBER trough is
where the decline stops; layoffs stop rising first. The 10 percent and four weeks are round values set before scoring and
are stress-tested in T7.)

## 5. The battery

- **T1** in-sample record at W12 (headline), W24, W4 [G1].
- **T2** blind walk-forward [G4-type]: every ρ is computed only from data and announced recessions known on its day (§3);
  the structure is fixed, so T2 is the same rule run causally. Scored on every recession whose W12 window starts after the
  families it needs have 10 quiet years.
- **T3** record theory: for each family, the expected number of years with a new quiet record per century, and the implied
  false-alarm rate of each design under independence, set beside what the record shows.
- **T5** simulated futures: 2,000 synthetic centuries assembled from the real quiet years (one-year blocks of all eight
  families, resampled together so their co-movement is kept) with each block's readings scaled by the measured ratio of
  successive quiet records; false alarms per century.
- **T7** stress of the shared line: every family's line × 0.9, 1.1, 1.2, 1.5; closer constants ±50 %.
- **T8** placebo chronologies: 1,000 boards of 13 fake recessions with the real durations placed in quiet time; p = (b + 1) / (m + 1).
- **Pre-mortem**: each design's highest reading (k-th largest ρ) inside every stall twin (1956, 1959, 1962, 1966–67, 1971,
  1976, 1979, 1986, 1995, 1998, 2003, 2011, 2015–16, 2019, 2022–23, 2025–26).
- **Knockouts**: each family dark in turn; each recession type of the atlas (claims-quiet, labor-shortage, calm market).
- **Test the tests**: the Sahm rule, the SOS rule, Michaillat–Saez and the 10y–3m curve on this harness first.

## 6. Selection rule and pass/fail

Choose by T2: fewest false alarms, then most recessions inside W12, then fewest simulated false alarms per century (T5).
T1 is reported and never used to choose.
A design **meets the standard** only if: T1 13/13 inside W12 with 0 false alarms and 13/13 closes inside ±31; T2 every
reachable recession inside W12 with 0 false alarms; T5 under one false alarm per century; T8 p < 0.05.

## 7. Predictions, stated before scoring

1. A11/A12 (any one family) will false-alarm many times (record theory: with eight families and about 60 quiet years each,
   several quiet records are broken per decade).
2. A1/A2 (any two of eight) will have fewer false alarms than A11 but at least one, most likely in 1998 (credit with the
   curve) or 2022–23 (the curve with markets), and will miss at least two mild recessions inside W12 (1960, 1969, 2024 are the
   candidates), because their families do not break expansion records by 31 days after the peak.
3. A9/A10 (two labor families) will miss 2024 (the Sahm gap reached 0.37 by 31 May 2024, below the 0.43 of December 1967 and
   November 2025) unless the claims or insured-rate family breaks its record in 2023 after 30 April.
4. No CL1-A design will meet the full standard. The value of CL1-A is the measured distance from it, family by family and
   recession by recession, without any fitting; it is the baseline every later outcome-guided design must beat in T2.

## 8. Variant count
